# 3D worlds: the ram and the valve

Two device worlds built from `src/three/dims.ts` (metres, the only source: every constant says `reported`, `derived` or
`estimate`) with the frozen ids of `src/part-ids.ts` (research §11): `ram` 13 parts, `valve` 14. `index.ts` exports
`createRamScene()` and `createValveScene()` (each returns its `SceneModule` plus its `model`). The site and the catchment:
`docs/3d-site.md`. Checked by `ram.test.ts` and `valve.test.ts` (ids, floating or buried parts, explode, NaN, section caps,
interpenetration of the sections, and that both worlds hold the very same valve).

## Files

- `common.ts` lathe, hex nut, thread, `paperFloor`, `FlowDots`, water material. `cut.ts` the section tool (`CutKit`).
- `merge.ts` fewer draw calls (`mergeStatic`, `dynamic()`), `lod.ts` the coarse copy for the distance (`farCopy`), `warm.ts` the shader
  warm-up (`installWarmup`), `framing.ts` the kit's overview camera and the label boxes replicated for the tests (see the last sections).
- `valve/` `model.ts` (`createWasteValveModel`: the valve and its animation), `parts.ts` (the 14 builders), `geo.ts` (derived
  sizes), `water.ts` (column, stream, spray), `explode.ts`, `bench.ts` (plinth, parallels, rule), `index.ts` (the world).
- `ram/` `model.ts` (the ram as one object, used by the `ram` world and, unregistered, by the site's pit), `body.ts`,
  `inlet.ts` (V1, strainer, inlet flange), `chamber.ts`, `fittings.ts` (gauge, snifter, V3, union, V2), `foundation.ts`,
  `water.ts`, `explode.ts`, `scene.ts`.

## One valve, two worlds

`createWasteValveModel({ cut, hardware, capId, floorAt })` builds the 14 parts once, in a frame whose origin is the centre of
the top face of the base plate (Y up, section plane z = 0), with its water, stream and spray. Used twice:

- `ram`: `valve.assembly` is ONE registered part, `waste-valve` (its 14 parts are its meshes; the water and spray are not).
  The plate carries the studs of the waste flange (`hardware: 'ram'`); the section caps belong to `waste-valve`.
- `valve`: the 14 groups are registered one by one, `hardware: 'bench'` (the plate alone), each cap belongs to its part.

Same builders and same `update(state, dt, frozen)`: the tests compare the 14 boxes of both instances to the micron in four
poses. Built in the closed pose; `liftFrames` are lowered by the lift when it opens, the fins turn `spinFrames` (hotspot
anchors never turn), the stack (stroke nut, weight, lock nut) rides `stackFrames` so that the stroke nut lands on the guide when open.

**Mechanics** (research §3; lengths are estimates from the fitting names, but the 7 to 8 cm nipple): plate with a Ø 26 bore
and four Ø 9 holes, welded 26×34 socket and bead, reducer 26×34 to 15×21, 15×21 nipple threaded over 7.5 cm with four ports
and its lock nut, female-female sleeve, thermowell guide (Ø 12 × 10 tube, collar whose end face stops the weight), seat milled
at 45 degrees in the foot of the nipple (drawn as its own ring), stainless head, M6 stem, two sleeves 9.9 mm in the 10 mm bore,
fins nut under the head, then the stack on the rod above the guide: the stroke nut (it rests on the guide; its height on the rod sets
the stroke), the rebar weight (it rests on the stroke nut), the lock nut (on top of the weight, it locks the stack). In the video's final
valve the machined weight is itself the main nut and there is no separate stroke nut: the lab keeps the two roles as two parts so that each
can be seen. Bottoms of the three, above the plate: 162, 167 and 227 mm, the top of the stack at 232 mm (`VALVE.strokeNutBottom`,
`counterweightBottom`, `lockNutBottom`, `stackTop`). Seat, head and fins sit inside the reducer: seen in section,
X-ray, isolate or exploded.

## The `valve` world

- Plate on two steel parallels on a wooden plinth, a 30 cm steel rule, the paper floor; metres, `minDistance` 4 cm. Hotspots:
  6 main (plate, reducer, nipple, guide, weight, stem) and 8 minor; none has `goto` (`partLinks` links `waste-valve`).
- **Explode** (`explode.ts`, computed from the open-pose extents, 12 mm between parts): lock nut, weight, stroke nut, guide, sleeves, sleeve,
  nipple, reducer and socket go up, the stem stays threaded through reducer, nipple and sleeve; seat, head and fins nut leave
  downwards between the parallels (the 18.6 mm head cannot pass the 15 mm bore). The assembly guide plays these offsets for
  the current part, so they are the insertion paths.
- **Section**: plate, socket, reducer, nipple (ports crossed), sleeve, guide, seat and head are cut and capped; stem, sleeves,
  fins, weight and nuts stay whole, as in a drawing. **Flux**: dots from under the plate through the seat out of a port.
  Water, stream, spray and dots are clipped by the section (dots 3 mm behind the plane, so those on the axis stay).

## The `ram` world

Its waste valve is the model above. New: `inlet-flange` (pipe stub, flange with hub, 2 mm gasket, four M10 bolts into the
strainer's flange; still starts at `STUB_X0`), `delivery-union` (three-piece union, compression nut, PE stub; the pipe still
ends at `OUT_X`), `foundation` (60 mm concrete pad under the feet with four M10 studs through the lugs, washers and nuts; its
top is `RAM.floorY`, its underside the floor of the world, `FOUNDATION_BOTTOM`). Explode (`ram/explode.ts`): all but the
foundation are lifted 70 mm off the studs, then go along their axis; the outlet valve and union follow the chamber.

## What `update(state, dt, view)` reads

`wasteValve`, `stroke`, `strokeCount`: stem, fins, weight, nuts (drawn closing 12 ms, opening 30 ms; stem turns 14 degrees
per stroke, at most 160 degrees/s; stroke drawn x4, 30 mm at most). `wasteJetFlow`: stream, spray, Flux speed. The ram also
reads the check valve, chamber, gauge, snifter and flows. `view.cutaway`, `view.schematic`; `slowMo` is not applied again.
While anything is exploded, hidden or wire, the valve is held open and the water hidden.

## Draw calls: merging, the coarse copy, the warm-up (fix wave)

Measured at the home view of each world, 1280 x 800, with `renderer.info` through `window.__lab.stage.renderer` (software renderer: the
counts are real, the frame times are not). One app frame, shadow pass included, no part selected.

| World     | Draw calls before -> after | Triangles before -> after        |
| --------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| site      | 831 -> 260                 | 485,539 -> 245,266               |
| catchment | 275 -> 187                 | 66,969 -> 66,969 (merged only)   |
| ram       | 333 -> 177                 | 124,457 -> 124,457 (merged only) |
| valve     | 72 -> 73                   | 53,495 -> 53,525                 |

- **`merge.ts`: `mergeStatic(root)`** bakes the static meshes under `root` into one mesh per material, in the frame of `root`. Meshes that
  differ only by their colour share one material and carry their colour in the vertices (the same pixels: a white material times the
  vertex colours of the linear colour it had). What moves, toggles or is reshaped at run time is flagged `dynamic()` and left alone: the
  stem, fins, weight and nuts of the waste valve (`liftFrames`, `spinFrames`, `stackFrames`, `rodTail`), the needle of the gauge, the disc
  and spring of the check valve, the stem and wheel of the gate valve, the lever of the drain, the gutter and its splash stone (laid again
  on the ground when the landscape moves), the wet gravel, the water and the jets. A material that the section tool clips is merged by
  identity only (it has a state of its own); caps and transparent meshes are never merged; a part is merged BEFORE it is registered (the
  registry collects the meshes of a part when it is added): in `ctx.add` for the ram (the waste valve keeps its fourteen sub-parts: the
  `valve` world and its tests compare them one by one) and at the end of `createCatchmentModel`; the site merges its tank and spring
  frames, house, tower, pit and the stones of the waste drain.
- **The rule for a new mesh that moves**: flag it `dynamic()`. `merge.test.ts` catches the one that was forgotten: it builds each world with
  and without merging (`MERGE.enabled`), drives both through the same states (running, slow motion, section, flows, explode, a hidden part,
  a stalled ram, new fall, drive length and head) and compares every triangle in world space (position to 0.1 mm, colour, shadow flags).
  It found the splash stone of the gutter.
- **`lod.ts`: `farCopy(model, { cell, minRadius })`** is a coarse frozen copy for the distance: the model baked in its present pose, the vertices
  in the same 12 mm cube welded (vertex clustering), merged by material, the bolts and threads (under 12 mm) gone. The site shows the ram
  in its pit through a `THREE.LOD`: the detailed, animated model under 9 m, the copy beyond (60 000 triangles in the colour pass and again
  in the shadow pass become 8 000 and 7 000). The copy shares the materials, so the water level and the clipping still follow; its
  water is a second copy toggled with the water of the model. A cell is under a pixel from 9 m: nothing changes on screen.
- **`warm.ts`: `installWarmup(module, probes)`** (every world calls it last): the first use of the section ("Coupe"), of X-ray or isolate, of
  the guide's wire frames and of "Flux" compiled shaders in the middle of the interaction (17 programs in the catchment, 514 ms in the
  software renderer). At the first idle moment after a world is drawn, the module draws itself three times into an 8 x 8 render target:
  section and flows on, every part a ghost, every part a wire frame, and puts everything back (display, shadow flags, visibility). It needs
  nothing from the kit: a probe mesh of the world hands over the renderer and the camera at its first draw. The module also has `warm(renderer,
camera)` (once only) and `warmed`; a kit that offered `module.prepare?.(renderer, camera)` would call it. What the lab cannot warm: the
  OutlinePass of the kit (its first selection compiles its own materials).

## The labels of the default views (fix wave)

`framing.ts` replicates the kit's overview camera (`Stage.frameAll`: the authored pose of the site, backed off on a narrow view; the
bounding sphere of the visible parts at `homePadding` for the other worlds) and the box of a label (`.hotspot`: a 14 px dot centred on the anchor,
then the name in 10.5 px capitals, 6.93 px per character, left aligned). `labels.test.ts` projects the anchors through it for the five
sizes of the 3D view that the three layouts give (842 x 678 and 842 x 736 at 1280 x 800, 380 x 902 at 768 x 1024, 390 x 632 and 390 x 420 on
a phone) in French and in English, and requires, for the labels shown in "Principales" mode (every hotspot that is not `minor`): at most six,
none over another (3 px apart), none outside the view, none under the toolbar (96 px at 842 wide, 130 px at 380, 92 px on a phone). Every
dot lies on its part (within 3 cm in the valve, 4 cm in the ram, 1.3 m in the catchment, 1.5 m in the site) and every label is the name of
its sheet or a shortened form of it (its words, in order, are words of the sheet's name).

| World     | Main labels (shown by default)                                        | Minor (shown with "Toutes" or when selected)                                                |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| site      | fosse du bélier (links to the ram), delivery pipe, water tower, house | spring, buffer tank, drive pipe, waste-water drain                                          |
| catchment | spring box, gravel filter, tank shell, overflow, intake screen        | feed pipe, lid, gate valve (links to the site), drain                                       |
| ram       | ram body, inlet valve V1, waste valve, air chamber, gauge             | delivery valve, snifter, outlet valve V3, anchor block, Y-strainer, flange, union, drain V2 |
| valve     | base plate, reducer, nipple, guide, weight, stem                      | seat, head, socket, sleeve, stem sleeves, fins, stroke nut, lock nut                        |

Why some labels are minor: a label runs to the right of its dot, so on the 380 px view of a tablet a name of 21 characters (184 px) fits
only if its dot is left of the middle, and the delivery valve, the outlet valve, the union and the drain valve of the ram are on the
right, whatever their anchor; the spring and the pit of the site are 33 px apart there and their names are 150 px wide. Anchors were
moved to the free side of their part where that helped (the house on its south wall, the delivery pipe on the first diagonal of its
route, the waste valve on its weight). Labels follow the glossary and the sheets (ram pit = « fosse », Y-strainer, ram body, delivery
valve).
